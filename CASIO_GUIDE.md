# ⌚ Radio-Controlled Watch Compatibility & Setup Guide
*(Casio MultiBand 6, Citizen Eco-Drive, Seiko Radio Wave, Timex, Braun, Junghans, etc.)*

> [!NOTE]
> **Universal Compatibility Notice (Not Just for Casio!):**  
> While this guide specifically references **Casio MultiBand 6 (Wave Ceptor)** modules—simply because that is what I own, collect, and am most familiar with—**this transmitter and these principles apply to *every* radio-controlled / atomic watch on the market** (including **Citizen Eco-Drive Radio-Controlled**, **Seiko Radio Wave Control**, **Timex**, **Junghans Mega**, **Braun**, and standalone atomic desk/wall clocks).
> 
> All of these timepieces listen to the exact same authentic low-frequency radio protocols (**BPC, WWVB, MSF, DCF77, JJY40, JJY60**) generated by this ESP32 beacon. If you own a Citizen, Seiko, Timex, or another brand, the beacon will broadcast the exact signal your watch expects. However, because every manufacturer designs their own user interface, button shortcuts, and regional radio-lock firmware differently, **you will need to consult your specific watch manual** for how to initiate manual synchronization and how your watch handles Home City/timezone settings across different regions.

---

## 🚫 The #1 Trap: Why Your Watch Won't Sync (Regional Radio Disabling)

Almost all modern atomic watches have regional firmware rules hardcoded into their microcontrollers:
> **If your watch's "Home City" or timezone is set to a city outside the official transmission towers (such as `SIN` Singapore, `BKK` Bangkok, `DXB` Dubai, or `SYD` Sydney), the watch firmware completely disables its internal radio receiver to save battery.**

Even if the transmitter is placed right next to the watch, the watch will **never attempt automatic nightly synchronization**, and pressing the manual sync button will immediately beep and show an error (`ERR` or `NO`) without even trying to listen!

### The Solution: Use a Compatible Home City / Timezone
To enable radio reception, your watch's **Home City** must be set to a city located in one of the 6 official radio broadcast zones.

## 🔄 How to Keep Local Time & Radio Sync (Home/World Time Swap)

If you live in a city like **Singapore (`SIN`, UTC+8)**, your local time is identical to **Hong Kong (`HKG`, UTC+8)** or **Beijing (`BJS`, UTC+8)**:
* Simply set your watch's Home City to **`HKG`** or **`BJS`**.
* The watch will display the exact local Singapore time, and MultiBand 6 radio reception will be **100% unlocked**!

### For Cities with Non-Radio Timezone Offsets (e.g. UTC+3, UTC+7, UTC+10)
If your local city has a different UTC offset than any official radio station:

1. Keep the ESP32 Beacon's **Timezone Offset** set to `0` (Native Time).
2. Set your watch's **Home City** to a station transmitter city (e.g., `HKG` for BPC or `BER` for DCF77).
3. Set your watch's **World Time (WT)** to your actual destination city (e.g. `DXB` UTC+4 or `SYD` UTC+10).
4. Most modern Casio modules feature a **Home/World Time Quick-Swap shortcut**:
   * **Module 3159 / 3495 (GW-M5610, GW-5000):** In World Time mode, press **`[Adjust]` + `[Light]`** simultaneously to swap Home and World Time instantly.
   * **Module 3293 / 3410 (GW-9400 Rangeman):** In World Time mode, hold **`[Adjust]`** and press **`[Light]`**.
   * Both your primary display and internal UTC baseline will remain mathematically accurate!

## 📡 Understanding Reception Displays Across Different Brands

Different manufacturers indicate signal strength and reception status in different ways:

### 1. Casio Digital & MultiBand 6 (G-Shock / ProTrek / Lineage)
When triggering manual sync (usually by holding the bottom-right button for 2–3 seconds):

```
       [ L3 ]   <-- Signal strength: L1 (Weak), L2 (Medium), L3 (Strong)
      RC !      <-- Flashing: Watch is searching and locking carrier phase
      - - - -
```
* **`RC!` or `READY` Flashing:** The watch has detected the carrier frequency and is locking frame phase.
* **`L1` $\rightarrow$ `L2` $\rightarrow$ `L3`:** Signal strength progression (`L3` is optimal; timecode decoding cleanly).
* **`GET` (Success):** The timecode passed parity verification. The watch beeps and updates.
* **`RCVD` Badge:** Confirms successful radio reception within the last 24 hours.

### 2. Citizen Eco-Drive Radio-Controlled (Promaster, Attesa, Chronomaster)
* Hold the lower right button (usually button `A` or `B`) for 2–3 seconds.
* The second hand will sweep to **`RX`** (Ready to receive).
* During reception, the second hand indicates signal strength: **`H`** (High), **`M`** (Medium), or **`L`** (Low).
* Upon completion, the second hand sweeps to **`OK`** (or **`NO`** if reception failed). Consult your Citizen caliber movement number (engraved on the caseback, e.g. `H800`, `H145`, `E660`) for your specific caliber manual.

### 3. Seiko Radio Wave Control (Brightz, Astron Radio Wave, Spirit)
* Hold the manual reception button for 3 seconds. The second hand moves to the **`Y` (Yes / OK)** or **`N` (No)** position to indicate previous sync status, then sweeps to **`RX`** to begin reception.
* When synced, the second hand confirms by pointing to `Y`.

### 4. Junghans, Braun, & Atomic Wall Clocks
* Digital atomic wall and desk clocks (Braun, AcuRite, Marathon, Oregon Scientific) feature a flashing radio tower / wave icon. Once the 60-second frame is decoded without parity errors, the wave bars turn solid.

## 🧲 Physical Placement & Antenna Alignment

Inside every MultiBand 6 watch is a miniature **ferrite rod antenna**. For maximum electromagnetic coupling:

* **Antenna Position in the Watch:**
  * **Casio G-Shock Squares (5600 / 5610 / 5000):** The ferrite rod is horizontally aligned across the **top of the watch (12 o'clock)**.
  * **Casio Round Cases (6900 / Rangeman / Mudman):** Usually along the **12 o'clock** or **9 o'clock** edge.
* **Optimal Alignment:**
  * Position the top of the watch (**12 o'clock**) pointing directly towards the side or face of the **16×18 mm inductor**.
  * Keep the distance between **10 cm and 60 cm**. *(Placing the watch touching the inductor directly can occasionally overdrive the sensitive RF preamp; a 15–30 cm spacing is optimal).*
* **Noise Avoidance:**
  * Keep the beacon and watch at least **1 metre away** from active laptop chargers, OLED monitors, and fluorescent ballasts, which produce LF electromagnetic interference.

## 🌙 Automatic Overnight Synchronization Schedule

Casio watches automatically wake up during the night to synchronize when atmospheric noise is lowest:

| Attempt | Local Watch Time |
| :---: | :---: |
| 1st Auto Receive | **2:00 AM** |
| 2nd Auto Receive | **3:00 AM** |
| 3rd Auto Receive | **4:00 AM** |
| 4th Auto Receive | **5:00 AM** |
| 5th Auto Receive (Optional) | **Midnight (12:00 AM)** |

Once a single auto-receive succeeds, the watch stops searching for the rest of the night. 

> [!TIP]
> The ESP32 Beacon web dashboard comes pre-configured with a default broadcast window starting at **01:55 AM**, perfectly covering the Casio 2:00 AM auto-sync window!
